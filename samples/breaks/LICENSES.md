# samples/breaks — where these came from

**Every file here was generated, not sampled.** No third-party recording ships
in this folder, so there is no licence to chase and no uploader's claim to
trust.

Generated by `tools/gen_breaks.py` with **stable-audio-open-1.0**
(Stability AI), run locally — the same pipeline and the same conventions as the
phrase shelf in `samples/`, see `tools/gen_samples.py`. Each prompt asks for an
*original performance in a style*, never for a specific record. The prompt,
tempo, bar count and seed for every file are in `tools/gen_breaks.py`, so any
of them can be reproduced or re-rolled.

## Why generated, and not sourced from a free-sample site

Gad asked for classic breaks — amen, jungle, 90s hip hop. **Those recordings
are not free**, and the free-sample sites do not make them free:

- The **Amen** break is owned by Richard Spencer / The Winstons and is
  unexpired. A producer sampling it sits in a grey area only because the
  statute of limitations ran out on old infringements; **redistributing the
  recording is a different act**, and TEN ships from a public repo.
  *Funky Drummer* is James Brown's, *Think* is Lyn Collins'.

- **Freesound is case by case.** The same site hosts DigitalUnderglow's
  *"classic acoustic drum breaks, run through a reel-to-reel tape recorder"* —
  the uploader's own playing, genuinely licensable — and OaSyntax's "Jungle
  Breaks", whose own descriptions read *"the 'amen' break by The Winstons"* and
  *"the 'funky drummer' break by James Brown"*. Same badge, opposite legality.

- **Creazilla was checked and rejected.** It stamps every item
  *"Public Domain (CC0) … No attribution required"*, but its stated Source is
  Freesound.org — it is a mirror, and it inherits the mislabelling wholesale.
  The item inspected was itself derived from *BandLab's* breakbeat pack, and
  its own "more like this" rail is a wall of Amen rips under that same badge,
  including one reading *"The famous 'amen break' by 'the winstons' cutted."*

- **Selekt Audio could not be verified.** Behind a Vercel bot checkpoint in the
  browser and 403 to plain fetch, and bot detection is not something to work
  around. Their claims — CC0/public domain, screened against a commercial
  recording database, a licence certificate per download — are the most
  credible of the three *as claims*, but claims are not terms.

**The rule this folder is built on: a CC0 tag is a claim by an uploader, not a
verified fact.** For the most-sampled recordings in history that claim is wrong
more often than it is right.

If a properly-cleared break pack turns up later — a Selekt certificate, or
DigitalUnderglow's own recordings checked sound by sound — it can sit beside
these with its licence named on its own line here.

## The files

**Nine breaks**, each trimmed to a whole number of bars at its own tempo so the
loop lands on the grid. Tagged `kind:'loop'`, `cat:'perc'` on the **phrase**
shelf, so they appear under the audio channel's `perc` type filter and never in
a sample op.

    jungle / dnb, 170bpm, 4 bar    junglechop  jungleroll  ragga  stepper
    boom bap / funk, 2 bar         boombap 90   hardknock 90   dustyswing 90
                                   lofibreak 90   bongobreak 100

**THREE WERE CUT, and they are cut rather than shipped quiet.** The first pass
rendered twelve; `darkcore`, `ridebreak` and `funkbreak` came back with ONE
onset each — measured, and the shape says what went wrong:

      darkcore    onsets 1   rms 0.241   crest  3.6    a distorted WASH
      ridebreak   onsets 1   rms 0.032   crest 27      near-silent, one hit
      funkbreak   onsets 1   rms 0.036   crest 23.8    the same

against 10-37 onsets and crest 5.5-13 on the nine that stayed. All three
prompts leaned on texture words — "distorted edges", "heavy on the ride",
"tight pocket" — and gave the model nothing rhythmic to hold, so it rendered a
TONE instead of a groove.

Their prompts are already rewritten in `tools/gen_breaks.py` to name the
pattern out loud, with fresh seeds in `SEED`. The re-roll did not finish: the
render degraded from 1.06s/step to 49s/step partway through, ~50x, under
memory pressure from a second model load. Re-run on a quiet machine:

    python3 tools/gen_breaks.py darkcore ridebreak funkbreak

and check them with the same measurement before keeping them — onsets in the
double figures, crest between about 5 and 13.
